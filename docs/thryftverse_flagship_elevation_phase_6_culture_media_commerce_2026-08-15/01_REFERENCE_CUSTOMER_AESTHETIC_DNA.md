## What the supplied grids actually communicate

The references are valuable because they show the customer's **media behaviour**, not a UI screenshot to copy.

### Repeated subjects
- private hotel/villa interiors;
- beach clubs, water, coastal travel;
- horses/equestrian environments;
- yachts/boats;
- luxury and performance cars;
- private aviation;
- Hermès/Birkin-like bags;
- tailoring and understated clothing;
- art/architecture;
- food/table culture;
- nighttime hospitality/events;
- candid portraits and social moments.

### Repeated composition
- centre or near-centre subject with substantial environment;
- vertical phone photography;
- strong horizons/architecture;
- low-light warm rooms;
- dark silhouettes;
- occasional overexposure/haze;
- imperfect crop used as authenticity rather than defect;
- little explicit product photography in white studio space.

### Repeated status language
The status signal is rarely “this object is expensive”.
It is:
- location;
- access;
- routine;
- companions;
- architecture;
- service/hospitality;
- materials;
- hobbies;
- transport;
- subtle product presence.

That means the app should not inject loud monetary or “luxury” chrome into every social image.

## Quantitative colour/tonality snapshot

Sampled reference composites:
- image count: **13**
- median saturation: **0.218**
- median brightness/value: **0.384**
- average very-dark pixel fraction: **33.0%**
- average very-bright fraction: **11.1%**

The practical implication is a **neutral/dark-capable canvas** with colour coming from content.

## Phase 6 visual character

Use these words:
- editorial;
- private;
- cinematic;
- tactile;
- discreet;
- modern;
- cultured;
- precise;
- international;
- effortless.

Avoid:
- “black-and-gold luxury”;
- glass everywhere;
- glowing AI controls;
- ornamental serif on all commerce UI;
- fake exclusivity badges;
- excessive animation;
- giant metric dashboards.

## UI response to this media culture

The application shell should disappear around media:
- fine hairlines;
- neutral blacks/off-whites;
- small iconography;
- real typography contrast;
- highly polished gestures;
- high-resolution image handling;
- exact transaction details only at the decision point.

## Ethical/product caution

Do not hard-code a class identity such as “posh kids” into ranking or access. The product can be designed around **high-aesthetic, high-trust commerce behaviours** without discriminating by perceived social class, nationality, race or protected characteristics.

Use taste/commerce intent, category expertise and seller quality — not inferred wealth — as product signals.
